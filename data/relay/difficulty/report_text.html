1 · Detecting off-policy text

Off-policiness shows up in whole turns, not in single tokens

Averaged over a turn, the calibrated measures pick the teacher's text out from RLVR1's own replies 82–91% of the time. Token by token, no measure gets past 58%.

For every teacher turn I sampled 8 replies from the student at the same point in the episode and asked how often a measure scores the teacher's turn as more foreign than the student's own reply. Raw NLL does worst, because the student's own samples at temperature 1 are noisier than the teacher's text. The measures that subtract the student's own uncertainty (surprisal − entropy, its standardized form z, and the turn-level D) fix that.

At the token level every measure sits between 0.42 and 0.58, in reasoning, prose and commands alike, for both students and both views. Ranking tokens by surprise finds where the student is uncertain, not where the teacher departs from it.

With earlier reasoning dropped, turn length alone separates 09-21's replies from the teacher's (0.69) better than any likelihood measure.

2 · Two students, one teacher

The teacher is off-policy for RLVR1 and on-policy for 09-21, which matches how SFT went for each

With D, the teacher's turns look as typical to 09-21 as its own replies, and clearly foreign to RLVR1.

If this holds on more students, a D score on 20 episodes (under one node-hour per student) would tell us whether a teacher's traces will help a student before any training. Dropping the earlier reasoning makes the teacher look more foreign to both students, because the later text follows a plan the student can no longer see.

Two students make this a hypothesis, not a rule. Raw NLL for 09-21 is 0.33, meaning the teacher's text is more predictable to 09-21 than the student's own samples are.

3 · Picking spans to teach

Inside a turn, the gap to the student's top choice picks the spans worth teaching

Judged blind, 19 of the gap's top 25 spans are knowledge, strategy, reading tool output or earlier reasoning. The z-score and p-value manage 10.

I took each measure's top 25 tokens (one per turn) with their context and had Opus label them without knowing which measure picked them. The z-score is good at telling whole turns apart but bad at picking spans. Dividing by the student's spread inflates positions where the student is nearly certain, so its top tokens land on whitespace, code layout and teacher mistakes.

The first tokens of a turn are a cheap second signal. 18 of their 25 are worth teaching, 13 of them the teacher reading the tool output it just got, and none are invented specifics.

What the gap finds, and what the z-score finds

Token rankings agree across the two students and both history views (rank correlation 0.83–0.98), so a span list built with 09-21 carries over to RLVR1.

4 · What SFT trains

The SFT recipe never trains half of what the teacher wrote

Only 47% of the teacher's tokens in these episodes are SFT targets. The rest is earlier-turn reasoning over 1,000 tokens, which the renderer cuts and masks, and it does not move at all.

I exported the clean-pair vanilla SFT (09-21, three passes over 1,877 CalibForge episodes that include these 20) after each pass and scored the same teacher tokens again. The trained and untrained split comes from the real training rows.

Most of the teacher's reasoning is never taught. SFTA has to settle first whether to train it or to target only the trained half.

5 · What SFT fails to teach

Among the tokens SFT does train, two-thirds of the hard ones stay hard

Of the trained tokens that start hard (under 10% probability), 66% are still hard after three passes, and nothing beyond starting NLL predicts which.

Starting NLL predicts which hard tokens stay hard (0.78). At equal starting NLL the best measure reaches only 0.57. The weak pattern points one way. Tokens the student was unsure about tend to stay hard, and tokens it was confidently wrong about tend to get learned.

So "SFT failed to learn it" is not a target by itself. Only 11 of the top 25 such spans are worth teaching (section 3). The rest are wording, invented values and teacher confusion. Filter with the gap first, then look at the tokens the student was unsure about. That is where extra context from a TA could help.

A token learned by pass 1 and hard again by pass 3 is rare (0.3% of tokens), so forgetting is not what keeps tokens hard here.

Next

What I would do next

  1. Decide whether SFT should train the masked reasoning. Which half SFTA targets changes everything after it.
  2. Score D for a few more students whose CalibForge SFT outcome is known (under one node-hour each). This tests whether a high D predicts that SFT will hurt.
  3. Run a TA pilot on the top-D turns. Give the TA the top-gap spans, have it write hints, and check whether the hints lower the student's NLL on those spans. No training yet.
Reference

Measures and method

Raw NLL
−log p of the teacher's token under the student.
Gap to the student's top choice
log p of the student's most likely token minus log p of the teacher's token. Zero when they agree.
Surprisal − entropy
NLL minus the student's entropy at that position. How much more surprised the student is than its usual uncertainty there.
Standardized log-prob (z)
Surprisal − entropy divided by the spread of surprisal under the student's own distribution (Fast-DetectGPT).
Turn curvature D
The same, summed over a whole turn before dividing. One number per turn.
Surprise p-value
−log of the probability mass the student puts on tokens no more likely than the teacher's.
Confident conflict
NLL weighted by how sure the student is. High when the token is unlikely and the student is confident (EAFT).
Rank, entropy
Rank of the teacher's token in the student's top 20, and the student's entropy itself.
NLL change without earlier reasoning
How much harder a token gets when the student does not see the teacher's earlier thinking.
First tokens of a turn
Position in the turn, earliest first. A baseline.

20 passing CalibForge episodes from Qwen3.8-27B with Mini SWE Agent 2 (seed 20261014, stratified by domain, no exclusions), 419 teacher turns and 493k teacher tokens. Students are Grug-67B-A2B Datakit SFT 09-21 and Antidoom RLVR1 step 12, scored teacher-forced with vLLM prompt logprobs (top 20) at 131k context, no truncation. Every teacher turn got 8 replies per student per history view (13,408 replies). The separation numbers are AUROC, with 95% bootstrap intervals over trajectories. Entropy is a lower bound from the top 20. Span labels come from six blind Opus judges over 174 windows. Total cost about 7.6 GB200 node-hours. The per-token scores for all 20 episodes are in the token viewer.
